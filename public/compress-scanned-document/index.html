Make a scanned document small enough to move.

Scanned paperwork often carries a surprising amount of image data. Whether it needs to fit an application upload or simply move faster through email, start with the real size constraint and keep readability as the non-negotiable.

Scans need different judgment than digital PDFs.

01

Check the source scan

Look for unnecessary resolution, blank pages and duplicate copies before compression.

02

Use the destination limit

Choose a target that satisfies the portal, application or sharing method without going smaller than necessary.

03

Inspect small details

Read names, dates, stamps, signatures and small printed type on the finished file.

If the scan was created at very high resolution, the cleanest path can be to create a better source scan at an appropriate resolution, then compress it. Repeatedly compressing a poor scan rarely creates a better document.

Scanned-document questions

Why are scans so large?

A scan is essentially a collection of raster images. High DPI, color capture and full-page photos can produce many more pixels than the destination needs.

Should I scan in color?

Only when the document needs it. For documents that are naturally black-and-white, a simpler source can be much more efficient.

Preserve the document. Lose the baggage.

Compress the scan, then verify the details that make the document meaningful.
