Private by default

The normal image compression step happens in your browser. That is useful when you're working with personal photos or documents you don't want to send to a generic compression service.

Choosing an image format

Format choice and target size are connected. Pick the format that fits the destination rather than converting blindly.

JPG

A common choice for photographs where compact size matters.

PNG

Useful for sharp graphics, screenshots, and images where transparency may matter.

WEBP

Can be compact for supported websites and modern workflows, but the destination must accept it.

Why two images of the same size can compress differently

A flat logo, a screenshot full of text, and a detailed photograph contain very different visual information. Pixel dimensions, color complexity, transparency, fine detail, and the chosen output format all influence how far an image can be reduced before it stops being useful.

PhotosFine detail often makes very small targets harder.
ScreenshotsText needs enough resolution to remain readable.
GraphicsSimple shapes may stay crisp at small sizes.

Image compression questions

Can every image reach 50KB or 100KB?

No. Some images need a larger target, smaller dimensions, or a different output format to remain useful.

Does Shrink upload my image?

The normal compression workflow is performed locally in your browser.

Should I always choose JPG?

No. JPG, PNG, and WEBP each suit different kinds of content and destination requirements.
